20 students · 4 crews · 5 roles per crew

Speaking game: The Time-Travel Incident Desk

Mission: Each crew investigates a time-travel accident and gives a spoken incident report. The group must tell a clear story using at least eight different irregular verbs, including five past-simple forms and three past participles. Use past participles after have/has/had or in a passive form.

Assign all 20 roles

Make four crews of five. Give each crew one scenario below, and assign one role to each student in every crew:

1. Time traveller
Explain where you went, what you saw, and what happened. Use at least two past-simple verbs.
2. Local witness
Describe what the traveller did and what you noticed. Ask one follow-up question.
3. Mission controller
Explain the instructions, identify the mistake, and say what the team had done before the incident.
4. Historian / scientist
Explain the historical or scientific evidence. Use two participles with has/have or in the passive.
5. News reporter
Interview the other four, then deliver a 30-second report using at least three irregular verbs.

Four crew scenarios

  1. Ancient Rome: The time traveller lost a device near the Colosseum.
  2. Age of Exploration: The crew met a sailor and brought back the wrong map.
  3. Future city: A robot hid the return key and the team missed its departure time.
  4. Ice-age rescue: The crew found an injured animal and had to choose whether to stay or return.

How to play (15–20 minutes)

  1. Preparation (4 minutes): each student writes three key facts and selects two verbs from the Verb Bank.
  2. Investigation (6 minutes): each crew member speaks in role; the reporter asks each person one question. Everyone must speak at least twice.
  3. Report (1 minute per crew): the reporter presents the incident. Other crew members may add one sentence each.
  4. Class challenge: listeners note the irregular verbs they hear and raise a hand if a form sounds incorrect. The crew can earn one bonus point for correcting itself.

Scoring: 1 point per accurate irregular form (maximum 8); 1 point if every student speaks twice; 1 point for a clear beginning, incident, and outcome. No points for repeating the same verb form.

Useful frames: “We went to …”; “I saw …”; “The device was hidden …”; “We have found …”; “The map had been taken …”; “What did you do after …?”
